Artificial intelligence · Small business

DukaAI

DukaAI is an AI-powered business-record tool designed to help small shop owners turn photos of handwritten notebook records into structured business information. I contributed as a member of the team.

Why it matters

Many small businesses rely on handwritten records. Converting those records into organized information can make it easier to review transactions and understand business activity.

Milestone

The team developed a working product during Hacktoberfest Hack Day Kigali and earned second place. This was a team achievement, and I am proud to have contributed to the effort.

Agriculture · Entrepreneurship

AgriTrust

AgriTrust is an initiative I co-founded with a focus on reducing post-harvest losses for smallholder farmers. Its direction is centered on practical ways to improve how produce is collected, handled and connected to markets.

Problem focus

When farmers cannot store, handle or sell produce effectively, valuable harvests can be lost and household income can suffer. Solutions need to be affordable and designed around the realities of farmers and local markets.

Current direction

The initiative is focused on exploring practical approaches and partnerships. This page will be updated as validated pilots, services and measurable outcomes become available.

Science · Water quality · Community innovation

Affordable Water Purification Research

This work grew from an interest in the challenge of access to safe drinking water in communities in Rwanda. Through science-club and school laboratory work, I explored a low-cost filtration concept using sand, activated charcoal and a polymer membrane.

Approach

The concept was intended to investigate whether locally accessible materials and a maintainable design could reduce visible impurities and selected contaminants. The project included early investigations connected with Nkombo Island and Rutsiro District.

Early findings and responsible validation

Project-reported observations included changes in turbidity, bacterial counts, plastic microparticles and nitrate readings. These are preliminary project findings, not independent certification that the water is safe to drink. Filtration performance must be verified through appropriate independent laboratory testing for microbial and chemical safety before any system is recommended for drinking-water use.

Impact figures: The project account has described three communities and approximately 340 people reached, alongside preliminary cost estimates. These figures are author-reported and should be independently documented before being presented as verified outcomes.

What I learned

The experience reinforced that innovation requires more than building a prototype. It requires listening to communities, measuring performance carefully, communicating limitations honestly and planning for maintenance and long-term affordability.

My longer-term ambition is to contribute to research and engineering work in Rwanda focused on sustainable water solutions and environmental chemistry.
