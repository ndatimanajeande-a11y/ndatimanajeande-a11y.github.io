# Chapter Two: The Weight of Doors That Did Not Open

There is a side of ambition that people do not always see. They celebrate admission letters, scholarships, awards and opportunities, but behind many achievements are unanswered applications, unsuccessful attempts, difficult decisions and moments when a person must find the courage to begin again.

My journey toward higher education was not a straight road. I applied to universities and scholarship programmes because I believed education could give me the knowledge and opportunities to make a meaningful contribution to my community and my country.

In January 2026, I submitted an application to Amherst College through the Common Application. In April 2026, I received the final notification. The outcome was not what I had hoped for. I responded respectfully, grateful for the opportunity to have been considered.

I also pursued international scholarship opportunities. In September 2026, my application to the Türkiye Scholarships programme was not selected to proceed. In March 2026, I applied for the Chinese Government Scholarship through Rwanda's Higher Education Council (HEC), but I was not selected. Later, in August 2026, I also applied for the Hungary–Government of Rwanda Scholarship through HEC. That application was unsuccessful as well.

One difficult experience involved my application to the University of Michigan College of Engineering. I understood the February 2026 outcome to be an administrative withdrawal because certain required materials had not been processed by the strict deadline. It taught me that submitting an application is only one part of the process. Applicants must monitor document delivery, confirm that institutions have received every required item and follow up early when something is missing.

## Setbacks beyond university applications

My development also involved leadership and learning programmes. Some demanding assessments and programme opportunities did not produce the outcomes I wanted. Balancing these processes with my responsibilities in AgriTrust Rwanda and my work developing hardware projects in maker-space environments was not always easy.

On August 15, 2026, I received an unsuccessful application decision from Hanga Pitchfest's sixth edition. I had submitted my application on July 23. On September 14, I learned that I had not been selected as a winner of the Oboe Learn-a-Thon, although I received a one-year Oboe Pro subscription at no cost. On September 24, I learned that I had not been selected for the Japan Water Forum Fund.

My pre-registration for the Africa CEO Forum 2026 was declined on April 25 because of limited capacity and selection criteria. The American Center TechRise STEM Training was postponed to June 25, 2026; that was a postponement, not a rejection.

These outcomes were not all the same. Some were scholarship rejections, some were unsuccessful competitions, some were administrative barriers, and others were delays or capacity decisions. Learning to distinguish them helped me respond more thoughtfully.

## The lesson of persistence

I learned that preparation must go beyond completing an application. Documents, deadlines, recommendations and follow-up communication matter. I learned that one route should not define an entire dream, and that time management is part of leadership.

I also learned that an administrative setback is not necessarily an academic judgement. It is important to understand why an application did not progress.

There were moments when I questioned whether I was doing enough or whether my goals were too ambitious. But I kept reminding myself why I started. I come from a background in which access to advanced scientific facilities and educational resources was limited. I know what it means to be curious about something that is difficult to experience directly.

I do not want to measure my potential only by the institutions that select me or the scholarships I receive. I want to measure it also by what I learn, what I build, the people I serve and the problems I help solve.

The doors that did not open taught me to look for other entrances. They reminded me that my journey is not defined only by where I was rejected. It is also defined by how I responded, what I learned and why I continued.
