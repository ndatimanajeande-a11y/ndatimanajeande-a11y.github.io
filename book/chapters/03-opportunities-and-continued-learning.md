# Chapter Three: Opportunities, Uncertainty and the Responsibility to Continue

Some opportunities arrive with clear answers. Others leave a person waiting, following up and trying to understand what comes next. My educational journey has included both experiences.

## Academic foundations

In December 2025, I took the SAT and achieved a total score of 1090, including 680 in Mathematics. My 2024 Rwanda Advanced Certificate of Education was in Physics-Chemistry-Mathematics, and I earned an A in Chemistry and an A in Entrepreneurship.

These achievements represent important parts of my preparation for further study.

## University opportunities

On October 1, 2026, I received an admission notification from the African Leadership University (ALU), titled “You've Been Admitted – Confirm Your Place.” On October 26, ALU contacted me with an offer to defer to the next available intake. I attempted to respond, but my reply bounced. The available correspondence did not establish the exact intake or confirm that the communication problem had been resolved.

This taught me that receiving an admission opportunity is not the same as completing every step required to secure enrolment. Communication, deadlines and written confirmation matter.

Another opportunity came through Chancen International Student Academy. After being on a waiting list, I received confirmation on November 6, 2026, that I had been placed in the January 2027 cohort at the University of Technology and Arts of Byumba (UTAB). I replied on November 7 with gratitude. The correspondence identified the institution and intake, but did not specify my academic programme or exact starting date.

## Continuing my studies

My education has also continued through the University of the People, where I am in Term 5 according to my study record.

In September 2026, unpaid assessment fees resulted in a financial hold on my account. I requested a fee waiver on September 4. On September 8, I received a response explaining that the outstanding fees were for CS 1111 and UNIV 1001, each US$160, for a total of US$320. The response stated that scholarships were not available to non-degree-seeking students and that extensions could not be granted. It also explained that a student could remain on hold for up to five terms before withdrawal.

The available email review did not establish whether the hold was later cleared. I include this experience because it shows that access to education involves more than academic ability. Financial circumstances can affect a student's ability to progress, even when the motivation to learn is strong.

I also explored opportunities through Neoteric Study in Türkiye and received registration-completion messages in October 2026. Those messages represented steps in exploring international study options, not proof of a confirmed university admission or scholarship.

## Other opportunities and constraints

On September 15, 2026, the American Center Kigali informed me that the list for that session of its Social Entrepreneurship and Business Development programme was full.

On September 28, I received a delegate-pass update from the Tbilisi Finance Summit that appeared to indicate participation was limited by venue capacity.

On October 9, I learned that I had not been accepted into the ALU Leadership Core Bootcamp scheduled for October 19–30. The reason given was that the cohort had been designed for a particular age group.

I also explored the Nova Future Summit and requested a fee waiver in September 2026, but the available email review did not identify a response.

I have also pursued opportunities at Kepler and NES Ruhengeri. I understand that I received acceptance opportunities but could not afford the required costs. The exact programme names, dates and financial amounts still need to be added from the original admission communications.

## What these experiences taught me

I have learned not to describe every registration as an admission, every invitation as an award or every delay as a rejection. Each opportunity has its own meaning, and each requires careful follow-through.

An admission notification, a cohort placement, a pending enrolment and a fully confirmed academic programme are different stages. Financial requirements, administrative processes and communication can determine whether an opportunity becomes possible.

My journey continues through these stages. I am learning not only how to pursue opportunities, but also how to manage them carefully once they appear. Persistence is not simply applying repeatedly. It is understanding the outcome, improving the next attempt, resolving practical challenges and continuing to build the knowledge and character needed for the future.
